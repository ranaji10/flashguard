# What was actually done, 26 August to 13 September 2026

*Source material for the proposal. Written 13 September at Ranaji's request.*

**This is not the proposal and must not be pasted into one.** It is a chronological,
honest record of what happened, with commits and numbers attached, so the proposal can be
written from evidence rather than recollection. Ranaji writes the proposal. Everything
below is raw material for him to cut, disbelieve, or use.

**How to read it.** Section 1 is the sequence. Section 2 is the measurements and what each
one is a measurement OF, which matters more than the number. Section 3 is the defects this
project found in itself, which is the most persuasive material here and the part most
likely to be left out by instinct. Section 4 is what is not true yet.

Every claim below is attached to a commit or a command that reproduces it. Anything that
cannot be is marked as unverified.

---

## 1. The sequence

### 26 August: a scope, and nothing built

`5856219` is the first commit: scope, protocol, prior art, a grant draft. On the same day a
guardrail test found that deny rules inspect the command typed, not what the command then
reads, so `bash script.sh` is opaque to the permission layer (`8cd0b07`). That is recorded
as a limitation rather than fixed, because it cannot be fixed at that layer.

### 29 to 30 August: the first bench run, and the first real defect

Two phones, a camera, a USB stick, an e-reader and a pre-Android handset, captured from a
bootable Linux session on an Acer. Thirteen records.

**The classifier could not tell a phone from a camera.** USB interface class `0x06` covers
both PTP (cameras) and MTP (phones in file-transfer mode). v1 mapped `0x06` straight to
`ptp_camera`, so two real Android phones across nine USB modes were all recorded as
cameras, and the coverage report said zero live positives about a session that physically
contained two (`d6a534f`). Fixed by resolving `0x06` by interface string then vendor ID.

**The re-run is a controlled before-and-after.** Same phone, same debugging state, same
cable, same machine: v2 said `ptp_camera`, v4 said `adb`. One variable moved. That single
comparison is the strongest piece of evidence in the dataset (`6f9b60f`).

**A device class nobody had thought of.** The old Samsung exposed a CDC AT-command modem,
USB class `0x02`. The classifier had no case for it and abstained rather than guessing.
None of the eight synthetic fixtures contained a device class their author did not already
know existed. That is the argument for real hardware, in one device (`531acbc`).

**Nine real USB descriptors published as fixtures** (`a3aeb4a`, `531acbc`). Six carry human
ground truth; three are kept as evidence but refused as assertions, because an abstention
cannot be ground truth: marking "unknown" as correct means "I agree you cannot tell", not
"unknown is what this is".

Also in this window: bash 3.2 portability enforced by a check after three
written-on-Linux-broken-on-macOS defects (`08c970e`), and the admission, committed, that
the verifier did not exist (`efec462`).

### 3 September: the open call, read the day it opened

NLnet Restack researched against live pages rather than memory (`5e545d0`). ELFA is dead,
NGI Mobifree closed its final call in December 2025, so Restack and CodeSupply are the
field. Restack chosen: its scope list names devices, full-stack security, reproducibility
and trust-enhancing technologies, and its ceiling is 150k per proposal against CodeSupply's
60k. Sixteen tracker notes answered, three decisions closed.

### 6 September: the machinery that keeps the project honest

The densest day. Four things, and they are the answer to "how does this project keep itself
honest" in box 11.

- **An index that fails the build when a document lies** (`b088ede`). Written after a run
  log was found claiming to cover a bench run that started four hours after the file was
  written.
- **A publication safety scan** over tracked files only, which is what would actually go
  public (`c8ea286`). It fails if anything under `library/` ever becomes tracked, which is
  the one rule the borrowed-material wall exists to enforce.
- **A blind review arrangement** (`ea41145`, `8e889d0`): a fresh session with no memory
  gets a diff and the suite, never a summary. Written because a stateful session had
  reported a false clean state three times, once insisting the verifier did not exist while
  `verify.py` sat committed at 118 lines.
- **The verifier exists** (`bebca9a`, `13dc923`). Minimal, and honest about it.

**The verdict gap, measured the same week** (`664a870`): the verifier and a human
disagreed on four of five corpus recipes. That number was recorded before anyone knew what
it meant.

### 7 to 8 September: the survey, and the finding that inverted it

The plan was to show that provisioning recipes upstream do not declare their prerequisites,
and then ask upstream to start. The survey was run properly: 90 OpenAndroidInstaller
configs, 556 postmarketOS `deviceinfo` files, 737 LineageOS metadata records, 1,383
machine-readable device records in total, with the checkout commit of each upstream
repository recorded so the numbers can be re-derived by someone who is not Ranaji
(`469c84a`, `a626e76`, `5d95124`).

**It inverted its own premise.** OpenAndroidInstaller declares its unlock prerequisite
structurally on 90 of 90 devices. postmarketOS on 0 of 556. So "recipes don't declare
prerequisites" is false for one project and true for another, and stating it as an
ecosystem finding would have been wrong.

**Which made the four-of-five gap a measurement of the wrong thing.** All four recipes a
human called unsafe were derived from OpenAndroidInstaller configs that DO declare the
unlock step. The information was never missing upstream; it was dropped in translation into
this project's own recipe format. The loss rate measures OUR format, not the world.

**And a claim was overturned before it reached the proposal.** The first survey called the
737 LineageOS install pages unsurveyable prose. They are template-backed: the shared
template branches on per-device variables and the front matter selects the matching
metadata record, so procedure shape is mechanically recoverable. Had that reached the
proposal it was a factual claim a reviewer could have knocked down in an afternoon, on the
largest device population in the ecosystem.

**Two harness defects found while finishing this work** (`c49838c`): the corpus replay was
passing each recipe its own target block as the fingerprint, so every recipe was checked
against itself. It was invisible because everything abstained anyway.

### 12 to 13 September: the verifier decides, and gets it wrong twice

Graded variant matching and evidence provenance landed (`f167e49`). Then, on the same day:

- **The commit that forbade false safes shipped one** (`06be780`, fixed `cf1b009`). When
  the variant was unknown and the device code was a declared alias, the branch was a bare
  `pass`, recording no reason. The verdict is cannot-verify if any reason abstains and safe
  otherwise, so the unknown variant contributed nothing and the answer came out `safe`.
  Reproduced first, then fixed, with a test that fails on the old code.
- **A tester naming a device was confirming its variant** (`a9c5b15`). `identity_source` is
  a real schema field carried by 8 of 10 records in the matrix, set whenever a tester names
  the device at capture. The verifier read it as human confirmation of the VARIANT. Almost
  every real record would have confirmed its own variant with nobody confirming anything.
- **A device serial had been in the repository for fourteen days** (`12ccbf0`, `5c87dc0`).
  A descriptor published on 29 August as one of "nine real descriptors, verified clean line
  by line" carried the serial inside the PRODUCT string. The privacy check grepped only
  lines beginning with `iSerial`, so it reported clean on every run. Fixed in three layers:
  masked at capture on both routes, checked across every fixture directory at commit, and
  scanned across every tracked file before publication with a planted leak proving the
  backstop fires. History rewritten and force-pushed; a clean clone confirms zero commits
  contain it.

All three were found by the blind review, on a commit that had already passed its own 23
tests.

**WebUSB proven on three platforms.** The probe reads the Nothing Phone 1 and the Samsung
A5 identically on macOS/Brave, Windows/Edge and Ubuntu/Chrome, ADB interface visible, no
driver install. The WinUSB blocker predicted on 3 September did not materialise for
descriptor reading. `tests/test-webusb-agreement.sh` asserts `classify.sh` returns the same
class from both routes on three real devices, so the two routes are proved equivalent
rather than assumed similar.

**And the boundary is real.** A Kobo, a USB stick and a feature phone were invisible to the
browser on every platform while both operating systems mounted them happily. Chromium
refuses whole device classes. The browser sees fewer device classes than the Linux kit, and
the proposal must say so rather than imply parity.

**A clean clone, for the first time** (13 September). Cloned from GitHub into a temporary
directory, hook enabled with the one documented command, full suite run: every check green.
Nothing was propped up by an untracked file.

**The repository went public** on 13 September, pull requests restricted to collaborators,
Discussions open.

## 3. What this project found wrong with itself

This is the strongest material here and the easiest to leave out. A proposal that describes
a defect it found and repaired reads as a project with a working immune system. One that
claims none were made reads as one that has not looked.

**One defect class, eight instances.** A mechanism that reports success while looking
somewhere other than where the problem is:

1. The classifier reporting zero live positives about a session containing two phones.
2. The publication scan exempting `docs/open-items-snapshot.md`, the one tracked file that
   could have failed it. An exemption inside a checker is not a mitigation, it is the
   checker agreeing not to notice, and it looks identical to a pass.
3. The path check that skipped the tracker, the file naming the most paths in the project.
4. The "moved recently" filter that emptied itself, where empty looks exactly like a sweep
   that changed nothing.
5. The browser's own stored copy silently overwriting the published page, so the same bytes
   rendered two different ways.
6. The version fingerprint hashing body LENGTHS rather than content, so a reword of the
   same length reported two different documents as identical.
7. The corpus replay checking every recipe against itself, invisible because everything
   abstained anyway.
8. The descriptor privacy check reading only `iSerial` lines while a real serial sat in the
   PRODUCT string for fourteen days.

**A second class, three instances: absence treated as a value.** A missing field meaning
"none required" rather than "not established" — in the derivation script, in the
prerequisite reader, and in variant matching. The schema now states it as a rule:
`not_applicable` means the field cannot apply, `unknown` means it applies and could not be
established, and an absent field is neither.

**Two false safes in the week `safe` first became reachable.** Both closed, both with tests
that fail on the old code. The first time a verdict can be wrong is the first time the gate
matters.

**The review arrangement earning its place.** On 13 September it produced five findings on a
commit that had already passed 23 tests. Four were real. The fifth was a false positive with
a real lesson: it flagged authorised decisions as unauthorised, because the packet
deliberately excludes the task that authorised them. That is a permanent cost of blindness
rather than a defect, and it is cheaper than the alternative.

## 4. What is not true yet

State these before a reviewer finds them.

- **`safe` is reachable and has never been returned on real hardware.** It requires a
  fingerprint confirming the required state, and 1 of 5 recipes has a captured device.
- **The corpus is five recipes.** The upstream survey is 1,383 records; those are different
  things and the proposal must not blur them.
- **The information-loss rate is weak as a measure of the corpus.** Nothing prevents the
  same author setting the human assessment and the expected verdict in one sitting knowing
  what the tool will say. It is defensible as a measure of the format.
- **The coverage floor has no number.** It cannot have one while decided share is bounded by
  a matrix gap rather than by verifier quality.
- **The tester programme has not started.** Ten addresses are expected before 18 September.
- **No upstream project has been contacted.** OpenAndroidInstaller states on its own README
  that it is not actively maintained.
- **The CC0 independent-authorship framing has never been read by anyone outside this
  project.**
- **Unreachable git objects may persist on GitHub after the history rewrite.** No forks, one
  collaborator, which is as low as that risk gets, but unreachable is not deleted.
- **`grant/` has not changed since 26 August.**

## 5. Things worth saying that are not numbers

- The tooling was written with AI assistance under Ranaji's direction and review. The design
  decisions, the safety properties and the proposal text are his. The verifier itself is
  deterministic rule matching over declared fields with no model in the decision path, which
  matters because Restack excludes AI projects that are not already in wide use.
- The producer, the reviewer and the analyst are deliberately three different systems, and
  the handoff between them is a commit, never a prose summary.
- The suite runs before every commit. Exit 1 blocks it, exit 2 reports and lets it through.
  A defect is the assistant's to fix and a decision is Ranaji's, and neither can hide behind
  the other.
- Nothing in the CC0 dataset is derived from GPL-licensed configs. The survey took counts
  and field names; config text did not cross the wall.

## Correction, 15 September 2026, and it changes an ecosystem claim

The survey reported LineageOS at 103 of 737 for unlock prerequisites and called it a floor.
That number counted the wrong thing, and asking LineageOS is what exposed it.

`custom_unlock_cmd` is a template override, set only when the correct command deviates from the
default `fastboot oem unlock` that the template already renders. Absence means the standard
command applies. What is actually declared is `install_method`, on **737 of 737** devices,
counted against the pinned clone. LineageOS declares its unlock procedure structurally for every
device it ships.

So the ecosystem line changes from "one complete, one partial, one absent" to **two complete and
one absent**, and postmarketOS at 0 of 556 is the only real gap. That is a cleaner argument and
it is now checkable by a reviewer in an afternoon.

**The number worth putting in the proposal is a different one.** On 348 of 737 LineageOS devices
(47 percent), the unlock is an out-of-band vendor process: the Mi Unlock Windows application with
an account and a waiting period, Motorola's web portal, Fairphone's support page, or Samsung's
Download Mode with no fastboot unlock at all. Each verified by reading the install template.

No verifier that reasons from device state can ever establish that such a prerequisite is
satisfiable, because the evidence is not on the device. That is a boundary on the whole category
of tool, quantified, and it is a better thing to say than any claim about coverage. It also sets
up the honest verdict: not "cannot verify", but "this device needs a vendor unlock that nothing
about your phone can tell me you have done, and here is what it involves".

**Also worth reporting: LineageOS were asked whether such a tool would be useful to them and the
answer was no.** One word, from a project director, in their development channel. That is in the
record because a proposal that reports the negative answer it went looking for is worth more than
one that claims universal enthusiasm. What it changed is the framing rather than the plan: the
beneficiary is not the upstream project, it is the person holding a drawer phone whom a
per-device maintainer cannot help individually.
